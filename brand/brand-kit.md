# INKWITNESS — Brand Kit

## 1. The idea in one line

A 1973 field notebook that learned to animate. Everything on screen looks like it was drawn and letterpress-printed by a patient human being, then photographed badly enough to feel real.

That is the whole defence against "AI slop": **one medium, obeyed without exception.** AI images fail when they mix media. Ours never mix.

---

## 2. Palette (use only these)

| Role | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Ink Black | `#101514` | line work, silhouettes, end card |
| Deep Teal | `#1B3433` | water, shadow blocks |
| Slate | `#3E5553` | mid-tones, cloth |
| Bone Paper | `#EDE4D3` | paper, highlights, type on dark |
| Amber | `#E7A34C` | **the only warm light.** Lanterns, lamps, gauges |
| Rust | `#A2452F` | one accent per frame. Wick's scarf, a lever, blood-warm metal |

Rules:
- **One rust element per frame, maximum.** It is the brand's signature, and the eye finds it instantly at thumbnail size.
- Amber is light, never decoration. It must have a source in the scene.
- No pure white (`#FFFFFF`), no pure black (`#000000`), no gradients that read as digital.

## 3. Typography

| Use | Face | Weight / size |
|---|---|---|
| Titles, thumbnails, episode cards | **Fraunces** | Black 900 or SemiBold 600, tight tracking (−2%), usually one line |
| Labels, episode numbers, credits | **Inter Tight** | SemiBold 600, uppercase, +14% letterspacing |
| Captions (if burned) | Inter Tight Medium | 42 px at 1080p, bone on 60% ink scrim |

Fonts are in `brand/fonts/` (SIL Open Font License, safe for commercial use).

## 4. Art direction — the non-negotiables

1. **Ink and wash only.** Charcoal, brush ink, cold-press paper grain, dry-brush drag, cross-hatching, slight ink bleed. Never 3D, never glossy digital painting, never "photorealistic with an oil filter".
2. **Visible paper.** Every plate keeps a warm paper margin or grain. We are showing drawings, and we are proud of it.
3. **One light per scene.** A lantern, a lamp, a floodlight, a shaft from the surface. Everything else falls to ink.
4. **No faces with features.** Crew and crowds are round-headed stick figures with dot eyes. This keeps the crew anonymous, keeps production cheap, and keeps Wick visually related to everyone else.
5. **Motion is slow.** Ken Burns only: 8–12% push or drift per shot, 30 fps, 0.9 s ink dissolve between plates. No wipes, no spins, no zoom punches, no stock "whoosh" transitions.
6. **Grain and vignette on the final pass**, not per image, so the film feels like one continuous piece of paper.
7. **Text never lives inside AI-generated art.** Titles, labels and thumbnails are typeset in code (crisp, editable, no mangled letters). This is the single biggest tell of slop, and we eliminate it by construction.

## 5. Voice

- Calm, low, unhurried. Reads numbers like they matter.
- Short sentences. One idea per sentence. Full stops are free; use them.
- **No** "you won't believe", "scientists are baffled", "what happened next shocked everyone".
- **No** speculation presented as fact. If something is unknown, say "we don't know" — it is more gripping than a guess.
- Always concretely grounded: dates, depths, distances, minutes.
- Address the viewer as an adult who is smart and tired, watching at 1 a.m.

## 6. Audio

- Narration only, no music bed (per channel owner's direction). Silence is a tool: hold 0.4 s after a hard number.
- Loudness target −16 LUFS integrated, −1.5 dBTP.
- If a music bed is ever added, it must sit at −30 LUFS under speech; no drums, no stingers.

## 7. Covers & thumbnails

- 1280×720, ink plate background, one subject, one number.
- Left third: Fraunces Black, bone, **3–5 words maximum**, stacked.
- Right third: the decisive image detail (a gauge at 12, a hatch, a lantern underwater).
- Wick appears at 6–10% frame height, bottom corner, as the brand's fingerprint.
- Ruthless test: shrink to 120 px wide. If the number still reads, ship it.
